Discovery & Problem Framing

From assumption to validated insight.

I ran a two-week self-audit on my own job search, logging every session, every task, and every minute to uncover the true Job-to-be-Done before writing any code.

Every morning I opened my laptop with dread. A dozen tabs. Endless scrolling. Three hours of coordination for every hour of actual preparation. I wasn't failing because I wasn't trying. I was failing because the system was broken.

Scope of this case study
This is a solo project with a sample size of one: me. Every insight below comes from my own tracked behavior, not an extensive multi-person research study. What this project does demonstrate: discovery discipline, trade-off reasoning under real constraints, and systems thinking carried from insight to shipped architecture. What it doesn't demonstrate: stakeholder negotiation or cross-functional influence (which I am happy to discuss from my enterprise work separately).
The Job-to-be-Done (as I experienced it)
  • "Help me identify the right opportunities without spending hours on coordination"
  • "Help me craft tailored applications that actually get responses"
  • "Help me stay consistent without burning out on repetitive tasks"
Hypothesis 1: Invalidated

"I need to apply to more jobs."

I started with the classic volume hypothesis: job searching is a numbers game. I optimized for throughput: more applications, more platforms, more tabs. The result was burnout, generic applications, and a 0% response rate on my spray-and-pray submissions (12 applications, 0 replies, over 4 days). The problem wasn't effort; it was signal-to-noise ratio.

Hypothesis 2: Invalidated

"I need a better tracker."

Next, I built a spreadsheet to organize my pipeline. It helped with visibility but created a new coordination tax: manual data entry, stale statuses, and decision fatigue about what to prioritize each morning. The tool was organizing chaos, not eliminating it.

Insight: Validated

"It's a systemic coordination problem."

I logged every session over two weeks and categorized every block of time. Roughly 70% of my time went to coordination (finding roles, cross-referencing requirements, tailoring documents, tracking follow-ups). Only 30% went to strategic work (researching companies, interview prep, building relationships).

Key Product Insight
The opportunity wasn't optimizing coordination. It was eliminating it through intelligent automation. The product wedge was a daily briefing that replaced roughly three hours of manual work with a three-minute review.

Strategic Decision Architecture · ADR Ledger

The hard architectural calls, and why.

Every high-leverage technical and product choice is an explicit bet with documented trade-offs, inspected risks, and reversal thresholds:

01 · ACTIVATION HOOK Observed N=1 Signal
Daily Briefing, Not Dashboard
~3 min Review vs 3h Drag
Push digest activation vs pull dashboard decay

A dashboard adds another surface to remember and check. A morning digest pushed directly to the candidate's inbox replaces decision fatigue with immediate action.

Considered: Pull-based web dashboard; autonomous auto-apply.
Risk: Auto-apply removes candidate accountability; dashboard gets forgotten.
Reversal condition: Evidence of proactive dashboard usage without notification nudges.
02 · QUALITY FILTER Empirical Calibration
ATS 80 Score as Bar
80+ Bar Optimal Signal-to-Noise
Calibration across borderline-scored listings

Scoring 70 created noise; scoring 90 starved viable opportunities. Manually evaluating borderline postings confirmed 80 consistently matched human judgment.

Considered: Threshold 70 (higher volume, false positives) vs 90 (overly restrictive).
Risk: Edge-case misses in unconventional job description formats.
Reversal condition: High false-negative rate measured across a broader job sample.
03 · QUALITY GOVERNANCE Architectural Invariant
Human Approval Gate
100% In-The-Loop Review
Zero blind autonomous submissions

The gate sits directly between tailoring and sending. This ensures the candidate inspects the actual outbound artifact, protecting personal reputation and application fidelity.

Considered: Uncontrolled auto-apply; gating prior to tailoring.
Risk: Review adds 15–30 seconds per application.
Reversal condition: Generative tailoring reliability proven over hundreds of live submissions.
04 · INFRASTRUCTURE LEVER Zero-Ops Invariant
GitHub Actions over VPS
$0 Infra Zero Maintenance Overhead
Built-in cron DAG scheduling & CI runner logs

Zero infrastructure management, transparent run logs, and free execution at personal scale. Product value lies in scoring logic and tailoring, not in hosting an always-on server.

Considered: AWS Lambda + EventBridge; always-on VPS with systemd cron.
Risk: Third-party runner execution queue latency.
Reversal condition: Need for sub-minute cron precision or enterprise compliance needs.
05 · SUPPLY CADENCE Directional Signal
Parallel AM / PM Pipelines
+40% Qualified Roles Lift
Sweeping mid-day ATS API updates

Job postings have a short half-life. A morning-only run missed afternoon ATS updates. Two parallel runs capture maximum yield without the engineering cost of streaming websockets.

Considered: Single morning batch; real-time websocket polling.
Risk: Additional Claude API token spend.
Reversal condition: Job boards adopting native webhook push alerts.

Technical Choice: GitHub Actions over Custom Infrastructure
Product reasoning: Using GitHub Actions meant zero infrastructure maintenance, built-in observability, and free scheduling. The product value lived in the intelligence and tailoring layer, not the orchestration infrastructure.
Technical Choice: Parallel Pipelines over Single Batch
Product reasoning: Job postings have a brief half-life. A morning-only pipeline missed afternoon updates from ATS APIs. Over the tracked period, the morning pipeline surfaced roughly 1.8 roles/day, while adding the afternoon run brought yield to 2.5 roles/day (an estimated 40% lift).

Daily intelligent briefings.

Commercial Viability & SaaS Dynamics

Market thinking, not just build thinking.

Although built for a sample size of one, exploring commercial viability highlights the natural tension between user success and recurring subscription revenue.

Customer ICP
Active job seekers running a high-intensity, time-boxed search applying to 15+ roles monthly. The product's value is directly proportional to search velocity.
Pricing Model
Monthly subscriptions are misaligned. Job searching is episodic: an 8-week search sprint pass reflects true usage better than a recurring charge users cancel as soon as they get hired.
Go-To-Market
Community-led and word-of-mouth: transparent build-in-public posts, targeted candidate forums, and referrals from candidates who landed offers using the engine.
Retention & Win-Back
Not continuous monthly DAU: win-back. The user returns for their next career transition 18–36 months later with preserved profile context and historical match data.
The Fundamental Business Tension
This product's best possible outcome—the user gets hired—ends their immediate need for the software. A standard SaaS retention metric actively opposes user interest. The proper North Star metric is Time-to-Offer or Qualified Interview Rate, monetized via sprint passes rather than prolonged subscriptions.

Executive Retrospective

CareerOS started as an automation project.

It became a lesson in product thinking and the realities of n=1 validation.
"What I built matters less than what I learned about problem discovery, trade-off reasoning under constraint, and being honest about what small-sample evidence can and cannot prove."
1
Validate before building. The cost of building the wrong feature is always higher than the cost of rapid testing. Two invalidated hypotheses saved weeks of misdirected engineering.
2
Constraints enforce clarity. Technical limitations (zero server budget, using GitHub Actions as a runner) yielded a simpler, more observable architecture than unlimited cloud resources would have.
3
Human judgment is a feature, not a bug. The approval gate was not a bottleneck; it was the exact reason I trusted the system enough to rely on it every day.
4
Systems compound; tools decay. A tool you check manually is a chore. A scheduled system that learns from corrections compounds returns across every cycle.
5
Small-N evidence is valuable when labeled honestly. Every number in this study originates from one person's search. Stating that upfront enhances credibility rather than detracting from it.
Product Leadership Takeaway
"Product Management isn't about finding the next feature. It's about finding the next bottleneck that's preventing customers or the business from succeeding."

Systems thinking, validated trade-offs, and human-in-the-loop AI applied to real-world ambiguity.
